How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Higher utility bills that point to a heater losing efficiency
  • Water temperatures that worry you before a health inspection
  • Equipment approaching the end of its expected service life
  • Scalding bursts or wild temperature swings at showers or sinks
  • Loud sediment noise from equipment that runs all day
  • Hot water running out during your busiest hour
  • Distant fixtures that take minutes to run warm
  • Rusty water or damp spots around the equipment

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Most of the failures we see could have been delayed for years with basic upkeep. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Inspect anode rods regularly, since heavy use consumes them faster
  • Keep recirculation pumps and check valves in service to avoid long waits and wasted energy
  • Set a flushing schedule based on usage; busy tanks collect sediment fast
  • Keep the area around the heaters clear for combustion air and service access
  • Have mixing valves checked so fixtures stay neither scalding nor lukewarm
  • Track temperatures at the dish machine and hand sinks as part of routine checks
  • Budget for replacement as equipment nears the end of its expected life
