How Richmond Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters last 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models usually run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs such as rusty hot water, popping and rumbling, dampness under the tank, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and much easier on you than an emergency one with water on the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both modest fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can wear out the valve and stress the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

What Goes Wrong With Local Water Heaters

Water heaters in and around Richmond face a predictable set of stresses. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, minerals can be part of the picture: dissolved minerals settle to the bottom of a tank as sediment, insulating the water from the burner, which produces the popping and rumbling many homeowners hear during a heating cycle. In tankless units, the same minerals build scale inside the heat exchanger, and that's why annual descaling is worth keeping up with where the water is hard.

We also see a lot of aging equipment. A standard tank water heater typically lasts 8 to 12 years, and by then the anode rod is often eaten away, leaving the tank open to rust. The area has a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, which means we service both sides: pilot assemblies, gas valves, and flues on gas units, and elements, thermostats, and breakers on electric ones.

Well water may bring sediment, iron, or sulfur into the tank, and a sulfur smell in the hot water often means a reaction with the anode rod that a swap to a different anode type can solve. Older installations often need code updates when a heater is replaced: an expansion tank where a check valve or pressure regulator creates a closed system, a properly routed T&P discharge line, drain pans where a leak would hit finished floors, and earthquake straps where local code requires them. Every one of these problems is fixable, and most cost far less to address early. If something seems off, a diagnostic visit from our team can keep a small problem from turning into a flooded floor.
