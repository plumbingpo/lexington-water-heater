Upfront Pricing

Straight Answers and Upfront Prices

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Lexington Water Heater provides honest, upfront pricing for every home, handling everything from a failed heating element to a complete tank replacement. Every visit begins with a thorough assessment of your water heater, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and the condition of the tank and valves, followed by a clear recommendation and a written price before any work starts.

Most water heater failures give quiet warnings long before the tank quits. Keep an eye out for hot water with a rusty tint, popping noises when the burner fires, a sulfur smell from the hot tap, or a hot water supply that runs out faster than before. Behind those symptoms are usually heavy sediment, a spent anode rod, or a failing dip tube. An early service call at the first sign usually costs far less than cleaning up after a failed tank.

How old is your water heater? The serial number on the label usually tells us. When a tank gets past the 10-year mark, it's worth planning a replacement before the current one forces the issue. As a rough guide, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. Our team will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is nearing the end.

Annual Flushing

Tank Flushing and Tune-Ups That Pay Off

Preventing a breakdown beats paying for one. That's the whole point of our annual maintenance. Once a year, we flush the sediment out of the tank, measure the anode rod, exercise the relief valve, inspect the venting and gas connections or the elements and wiring, and run a descaling cycle on tankless units, all tailored to the equipment in your home.

We offer flexible maintenance options for homeowners, with annual service for most tanks and more frequent flushing where the water is hard. This is especially important where mineral content is high, where sediment coats the tank floor, forces the burner to run longer, and shortens the life of the tank. Each home is assessed individually, and the plan is tailored to your heater's age, your household's hot water needs, and your local water.

Think of maintenance as protection for equipment you've already paid for. Each visit helps keep the tank clear of sediment, the anode rod working, and the relief valve ready to open, and that lowers monthly energy bills while reducing wear on the burner or elements. For most households, maintenance costs far less than replacing the heater ahead of schedule.

Sized for Your Household

The Right Size Water Heater for Your Home

When it comes to water heaters, bigger is not automatically better. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, how many gallons of hot water it can deliver in one busy hour, and for a tankless unit it's the flow rate it can sustain at your incoming water temperature. That's why we never just copy the gallon rating of the old heater. Instead, we look at how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have, and big draws like a soaking tub or a high-capacity washer.

We walk you through the options for every home, especially growing families with rising hot water needs. A 40-gallon tank that served a couple rarely holds out once kids are showering every morning, and a tankless water heater can deliver continuous hot water as long as it's sized for the flow. If you're weighing tank against tankless, or gas against a heat pump model, our technician will lay out the honest trade-offs: upfront cost, operating cost, gas line and venting requirements, electrical capacity, and space, so you can pick what fits your home and budget.

Repairs We Handle

Common Water Heater Problems We Fix in Lexington

A cold tap where hot water should be, a burner that refuses to light, a rumbling, sediment-heavy tank, a slow drip from the relief valve: every one of these problems has a different cause and needs a different repair. Lexington Water Heater diagnoses the exact fault before swapping any parts, whether it's an electric tank that trips its breaker, a gas heater with a failing thermocouple, a broken dip tube mixing cold water into the hot, or a tankless unit flashing error codes.

The correct fix depends entirely on the part that failed. On electric tanks, that usually means an element or thermostat. On gas units, it tends to be a thermocouple, flame sensor, or gas valve. Pressure spikes get solved with an expansion tank and a properly set pressure-reducing valve, and leaks at the drain valve or fittings get repaired before they spread. We also check the temperature setting, since water above 120°F can scald young children and older adults in seconds. A tank leaking from the steel itself can't be patched, and we'll tell you so. Any gas smell means leave the house and call the gas utility right away.
