Here is what shortens most water heaters' lives: corrosion from the inside and mineral buildup on the bottom, both of which are largely preventable. As water heats, dissolved minerals come out of the water and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer insulates the water from the burner, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it builds up around the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, is slowly used up. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Lexington, KY, we see this pattern every week, and most of it was preventable.

A Lexington Water Heater visit is designed to stop both of those processes early. We drain and flush the tank until the water runs clear, remove and check the anode rod and recommend a replacement when it is mostly consumed, and test the temperature and pressure relief valve to confirm it opens and reseats. On gas units we inspect the burner, pilot or igniter, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue baffle, and venting for proper draft. On electric units we check element resistance and thermostat operation and look at the wiring for heat damage. We check the expansion tank's air charge against your water pressure, confirm the thermostat setting is near 120 degrees to limit scalding, and look for early leaks at fittings and valves.

If you have a tankless unit, the routine changes but the goal is the same. Minerals coat the heat exchanger over time, reducing efficiency and eventually causing shutdowns. Our technicians connect a circulation pump to the isolation valves and run a descaling solution through the exchanger, clean the water filter, inspect the venting, and check the condensate drain on condensing models. Every visit ends with a clear report, noting what is healthy and what needs attention. Call (859) 203-0152 to book maintenance for your Lexington water heater.

When to Call for Water Heater Maintenance

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank
  • Rising utility bills that point to lost efficiency
  • Noises from the water heater that were not there before
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap
  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system
  • Moisture, drips, or rust around the base or fittings

The Benefits of Professional Water Heater Maintenance

A few maintenance tasks suit homeowners, and many do not. Keeping clutter away from the heater, checking for leaks, and noticing new noises are all useful. Flushing, anode replacement, relief valve testing, and burner or element work are a different matter. Hot water, seized fittings, brittle drain valves, and sediment that clogs the valve midway through can turn a simple job into a flooded floor. Relief valves on older heaters sometimes fail to close after testing. And gas-side checks involve flame quality and venting, which protect your household's air.

Professional maintenance also brings the right parts on the truck, including drain valves, anode rods and flexible anodes, relief valves, and meters for electrical testing. Tankless descaling gear comes along for those units. Beyond the tools, experience matters: an experienced eye can spot a corroding nipple, a sooty burner, or an expansion tank that has lost its charge long before any of them cause trouble. Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
  • Look around the base of the heater periodically for moisture
  • Make an annual flush part of your home maintenance routine
  • Treat hard water to cut down on sediment and scale
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
