A tankless water heater takes a different approach: instead of keeping a full tank of water hot around the clock, it heats water only while a tap is open. When water starts moving, a flow sensor notices, a powerful burner or electric element fires, and the water is heated as it passes through a compact heat exchanger. The benefits are real: no standby heat loss, hot water that does not run out during back-to-back showers, a wall-mounted unit that frees up floor space, and a service life that often runs close to two decades with maintenance. For many households in Lexington, KY, that makes tankless a smart upgrade.

The most important step happens before anything is installed. We total your peak demand in gallons per minute, such as a shower, a kitchen sink, and the dishwasher at once, and compare it against how much the unit must heat cold winter water. That tells us the real capacity you need. Next we check the supply side. Gas tankless units fire at up to around 199,000 BTU, so a half-inch line that fed an old tank is often too small. Condensing models vent through PVC or polypropylene and need a condensate drain, while non-condensing units need stainless steel venting. Electric units bring their own demands, often needing multiple large breakers and sometimes a service upgrade.

Every tankless install from Lexington Water Heater includes isolation service valves so future descaling takes minutes instead of hours, a clean gas connection with a sediment trap, correctly pitched and terminated venting, and a pressure relief valve piped to a safe location. If your water is hard, we talk through scale protection, since mineral buildup is the main enemy of a tankless heat exchanger. We can add recirculation for faster hot water at distant bathrooms, set the temperature, handle permits where required, and walk you through maintenance before we leave. Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • A water heater near the end of its service life, making this a natural time to switch
  • Renovation work that makes gas line and vent changes easier to fit in
  • Standby losses on a tank that sits idle for long stretches
  • More people or more fixtures than your current heater was sized for
  • A remodel that would benefit from a compact wall-mounted heater
  • Interest in a longer-lasting heater with replaceable parts
  • Hot water running out during back-to-back showers
  • An addition or guest suite that is a long run from the main heater

Act Before It Gets Worse

Tanks rarely fail all at once. They corrode from the inside for months, and the warning signs show up long before the floor gets wet. If you notice any of these signs, contact Lexington Water Heater at (859) 203-0152 for a prompt inspection.

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

Buying the right unit is the easy part. The other half is making sure your home can support it. Gas tankless burners can fire at close to 199,000 BTU, so gas line sizing, meter capacity, and pressure under full load all have to check out. Vent materials, lengths, and terminations are dictated by the manufacturer and local code, and condensing units add a condensate drain that should be neutralized. Electric whole-home units can pull more current than an older panel can deliver. Get any of these wrong, and the unit underperforms or becomes unsafe.

A professional install protects both performance and safety. We start with a real demand assessment, confirm the gas and electrical supply, and design venting that meets the manufacturer's requirements. The install includes service valves, a sediment trap, a correctly routed relief valve line, and a neutralized condensate drain where needed, followed by a full-flow test. Just as important, we tell you plainly if a tank, hybrid, or smaller point-of-use unit would serve you better. The right fit matters more to us than the type of unit.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Put these technician-approved steps to work at home:

  • Avoid cranking the temperature higher than you need; about 120F works for most homes
  • Check and rinse the small filter screen at the cold inlet periodically
  • Hard water shortens tankless life, so ask about scale protection
  • Descale the heat exchanger once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Protect outdoor or garage units from freezing, and keep power on during cold snaps so freeze protection works
  • Write down any error codes and call early rather than resetting repeatedly
  • Check the vent cap outside for nests, debris, or buildup
