How do you know it is time to replace a water heater rather than repair it? Start with the age. Tanks tend to wear out between eight and twelve years, and the serial number on the label dates yours precisely. The symptoms come next. A leak from the tank itself, rusty water from the hot side only, and loud sediment noise all point to a tank near the end. Parts like thermocouples, elements, and relief valves can be replaced on almost any heater, but a rusted-through tank cannot, and plenty of households in Lexington, KY tend to learn that the hard way, with a wet floor.

A new water heater should fit the way your household uses hot water today, not the way it did when the old one went in. That is why Lexington Water Heater asks a few questions first: who showers when, whether there is a big soaking tub, and how many fixtures run at the same time. Tank heaters are compared by first-hour rating, while tankless units are sized by gallons per minute at the temperature rise needed in the coldest months. Heat pump models get a check for space and airflow, since they pull heat from the room around them. We also confirm your gas line, vent, and electrical panel can handle the new unit, so there are no surprises on installation day.

The final stretch is where a careful installer and a rushed one part ways. Our technicians remove and haul away the old tank, install the new heater with new connectors and a proper shutoff, and leak-test every gas and water joint. The vent is sized and sloped to spec, the gas line gets its sediment trap, and the relief valve discharge runs to the right spot. Code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps are added where required, and the permit is handled when your jurisdiction needs one. A standard tank swap is usually done in a day. Signs You Need Water Heater Replacement in Lexington

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Rust flaking from the tank jacket or its connections
  • Repeated service calls on the same aging heater
  • Operating costs climbing on an older, less efficient heater
  • Water leaking from the bottom or body of the tank
  • Sediment noise that keeps getting louder
  • Less and less hot water, even after repairs
  • Rust-colored hot water while the cold stays clear
  • A heater older than most tanks last, usually eight to twelve years

Why Hire a Professional for Water Heater Replacement?

A water heater replacement touches more systems than most people expect. Gas work covers the supply line, sediment trap, shutoff, and a vent that must draft well enough to keep exhaust out of the house. On the electric side, the breaker and wire gauge must match the element wattage. The tank needs a relief valve of the correct rating piped to a safe discharge point, and depending on your home, code may require a thermal expansion tank, a drain pan, earthquake straps, or a permit and inspection. Tankless and heat pump models add their own demands: bigger gas lines, sealed vent systems, heavy electrical loads, and condensate drainage.

Professional service handles the full job, not just the tank. We size the heater by first-hour rating or flow rate, verify the fuel, venting, and electrical capacity, and add the expansion tank, drain pan, or straps your home needs. Every gas and water joint is leak-tested, gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check, and the old heater is hauled away. The result is a heater that is safe, sized for your household, and ready for inspection if one is required. If your current tank still has good years left and just needs a part, you will hear it from us directly.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Make sure an expansion tank is in place if your home has a closed plumbing system
  • Inspect the anode rod every few years; replacing it can add years to the tank
  • Start a flushing routine in the first year, before sediment hardens
  • Heat pump units need a clean filter and open airflow to run efficiently
  • Tankless units need a yearly descaling, especially where the water is hard
  • Write the install date on the tank so you know when to plan the next replacement
  • Avoid running the tank hotter than about 120 degrees unless there is a specific need
