Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • A complete loss of hot water throughout the house
  • Water at the tap that is suddenly scalding hot
  • Scorch marks, soot, or flames rolling out near the burner access on a gas unit
  • Water or steam coming from the temperature and pressure relief valve or its discharge pipe
  • An electric water heater that keeps tripping its circuit breaker
  • A rotten-egg smell near a gas water heater (leave first, and call the gas utility)
  • A puddle under the tank that keeps growing
  • Loud popping, banging, or rumbling from the tank that has gotten worse quickly

Why This Job Calls for a Pro

A water heater emergency stacks several hazards in one small space. There is water on the floor near a 240-volt circuit on electric models, or near a gas valve, burner, and flue on gas models. There is a tank holding very hot water under pressure. And there is a safety valve that, if it is discharging, is doing so for a reason. Homeowners sometimes try to stop the relief valve by capping it, crank on a leaking fitting until it snaps, or relight a pilot beside a gas smell, and each of those can turn a bad afternoon into a real disaster. The safest moves for a homeowner are the ones described above: leave for gas, then shut off water and energy, then call for help.

A trained technician brings the right order of operations and the right parts. Our team confirms the heater is isolated, tests for gas leaks with a detector and soap solution rather than guessing, checks household water pressure and thermal expansion when a relief valve has been discharging, and inspects the vent for proper draft before a gas unit is restarted. Just as important, we tell you honestly when a heater can be repaired and when it cannot. A leaking seam or rusted-through bottom cannot be fixed, while a failed valve, fitting, or element usually can, and the difference is often a modest repair bill versus the cost of a whole new unit. You get that answer before any work begins.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

A few preventive steps can help you avoid a cold shower or a wet floor down the road. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Leave clear space around a gas heater and never store flammable liquids nearby
  • Know where the heater shutoff and the main water valve are, and make sure everyone in the house does too
  • A simple water sensor on the floor beside the tank can warn you hours earlier
  • Put the heater in a drain pan piped to a floor drain or the exterior where the setup allows
  • Lift the relief valve lever once a year to confirm it opens and reseats, and call if it keeps dripping
  • Keep the thermostat near 120F to limit scalding and reduce strain on the tank
  • Drain a few gallons or flush the tank annually to keep sediment from cooking the tank floor
