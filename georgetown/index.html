Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Georgetown

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on descaling once a year in hard-water areas.

Age matters too. Most tank water heaters run 8 to 12 years, tankless units often reach 20 with regular descaling, and heat pump models typically run 10 to 15. If your heater is past that window and showing warning signs like discolored hot water, rumbling during heating, moisture around the base, or showers that go cold sooner, a planned replacement is almost always cheaper and far less stressful than an emergency one after the tank lets go.

Left alone, a small problem tends to grow. Consider a spent anode rod: swapping it out is a quick, inexpensive repair, but once it's gone, the water begins corroding the tank itself, and suddenly a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to save our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a surprise cleanup.
